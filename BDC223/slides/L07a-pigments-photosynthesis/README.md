# Lecture 7a pigment slides

Six replacement slides for the six supplied legacy images, in the same 16:9
Beamer/TikZ style as Lectures 6, 7b, 8 and 10. All charts, diagrams, text and
tables are editable in `L07a-pigments-slides.tex`. Build with `sh build.sh`.
The PDF and six 1920-pixel PNGs are produced from the same source.

## Sources and interpretation

- Content and the nine algal-group rows follow the six supplied teaching slides,
  formerly `BDC223_L06a_slide03.jpeg` through `BDC223_L06a_slide08.jpeg`.
- The historical pigment table attributes its examples to South and Whittick
  (1987). Group names and all rows are retained. The table is labelled as
  examples rather than universal diagnostic lists. Spellings are corrected to
  diadinoxanthin and siphonaxanthin.
- The absorption curves are original schematic Gaussian-band drawings, not
  digitised observations. Heights and widths are illustrative and independently
  scaled; they must not be interpreted as measured percentage absorption or
  pigment concentrations.
- Chlorophyll a maxima near 430 and 662 nm, and chlorophyll b maxima near 453
  and 642 nm, refer to pigments in diethyl ether. See
  [Chlorophyll Pigments and Their Synthetic Analogs](https://academic.oup.com/pcp/article/66/2/153/7738995),
  Table 1. Protein binding and the solvent can shift maxima; the lecture text
  distinguishes isolated pigments from pigments in living cells.
- The accessory-pigment panel shows representative carotenoid, phycoerythrin
  and phycocyanin bands. It does not claim that every accessory pigment fills
  the entire green region, or that every algal group has all these pigments.
  The phycobiliprotein bands are consistent with primary measurements such as
  [Phycoerythrins of the Red Alga Callithamnion (1981)](https://academic.oup.com/plphys/article/68/2/482/6078189)
  and [Association of phycoerythrin and phycocyanin (1981)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7260042/).
- Background on the distribution and functions of carotenoids:
  [Takaichi (2011), Carotenoids in Algae](https://doi.org/10.3390/md9061101).
